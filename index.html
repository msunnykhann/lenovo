01. Introduction

Lenovo’s journey began in 1984 within a humble Beijing guardhouse, where 11 visionary engineers founded "Legend" with just 200,000 yuan, driven by a mission to bring digital computing into every household. To scale globally and embrace future innovation, the company rebranded as "Lenovo" in 2003—a seamless fusion of their heritage ("Le" from "Legend") and future vision ("novo", the Latin word for new). Alongside this evolution, they introduced their iconic slanted rectangular logo, symbolizing dynamic speed, agility, and forward momentum.

02. The Lenovo Evolution

1984

Chapter 1: Out of the Guardhouse

Eleven visionary Chinese scientists, led by Liu Chuanzhi, gather inside a drafty, tiny security guardhouse in Beijing with just 200,000 yuan. They name their dream "Legend". Although their early attempts to distribute digital watches fail, they adapt, engineering a character-board that translates English OS into Chinese. The seed of a global titan is sown.

1992

Chapter 2: The Bento Box Innovation

Across the ocean in Yamato, Japan, IBM engineers design a mobile computer inspired by the simple elegance of a traditional Japanese Shokado bento box. Featuring a matte black shell and a glowing red TrackPoint button at its heart, the ThinkPad 700C is born. It goes on to redefine business computing and even journeys into outer space on NASA missions.

2005

Chapter 3: The Giant Leap

Legend (renamed Lenovo) acquired IBM's PC division. Critical pundits predicted failure; instead, it instantly forged the world's 3rd largest PC maker.

2013

Chapter 4: Standing at the Summit

Skeptics are silenced. Through relentless supply chain optimization and expanding its catalog with pioneering models like the convertible Yoga laptops alongside the enterprise-standard ThinkPads, Lenovo officially claims the crown: becoming the #1 PC manufacturer on Earth [Gartner & IDC, Q2 2013].

2014

Chapter 5: Building a Diversified Giant

Recognizing that the desktop computer is only one link in a larger chain, Lenovo makes two massive acquisitions in a single year: purchasing Motorola Mobility from Google to capture the mobile industry, and buying IBM's high-margin x86 server hardware business to anchor itself in corporate data centers.

2020

Chapter 6: Untethering the Laptop

Anticipating a world that works from anywhere, Lenovo introduces the Yoga 5G, the world's very first PC designed with built-in cellular 5G network speeds. Lenovo engineers successfully combine the computing power of a premium 2-in-1 notebook with the constant, high-speed connection of a mobile phone.
